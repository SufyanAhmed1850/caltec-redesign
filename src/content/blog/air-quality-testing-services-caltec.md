In busy industrial areas, air quality is easy to overlook. Production targets are visible every day; the condition of the air in a hall or inside a compressed-air line usually isn't — until it causes a problem.

## What gets measured

Air quality testing looks at particulates, moisture, oil vapour and other contaminants — both in the ambient working environment and inside compressed-air systems that feed packaging, pneumatics and instrumentation. The results are compared against the relevant standards, such as ISO 8573 for compressed air purity.

## Why it matters

- **People:** poor air contributes to respiratory problems and fatigue on the shop floor.
- **Product:** contaminated air can compromise food packaging, pharmaceutical processing and sensitive finishes.
- **Energy:** leaks and moisture force compressors to work harder, driving up running costs.
- **Compliance:** environmental and quality audits increasingly expect documented evidence.

## The compressor air audit

An audit takes a system-wide view. Instead of a single sample, it looks at pressure drop, flow, filtration performance and leakage across the network. The result is a practical list of fixes — often with a clear energy payback.
